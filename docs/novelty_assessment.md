# Novelty Assessment (interim)

Status: interim, 25 October 2026. Based on 24 screened sources, of which one has been partially read and one read through its authors' summary. Google Scholar, SSRN and JSTOR have not yet been searched directly. Nothing below is a claim of novelty. It is a map of where the project sits relative to what has been found so far, and what must still be checked.

## What is already established

| Topic | What the literature already shows | Key sources |
|---|---|---|
| RBI reaction function | High inertia and asymmetry in Taylor type rules; after inflation targeting, the RBI responds to its own inflation projections more clearly than to realized inflation; a hybrid rule fits best | Ahmad and Kundu 2022; Garga and Sengupta 2026 |
| Predicting central bank decisions | Ordered probit and ordered choice models with inaction bands predict hike, hold and cut decisions out of sample for Korea, the UK and the US | Kim 2014; Chevapatrakul, Kim and Mizen; Sirchenko |
| Machine learning for decisions | In the one emerging market study found, gradient boosting failed to beat a majority class baseline in walk forward evaluation for Turkey | CBRT SHAP study 2026 |
| RBI policy shocks | OIS based high frequency shocks for 115 RBI announcements, 2003 to 2020, publicly released | Lakdawala and Sengupta 2021 |
| Nepal transmission | Bank lending and interest rate channels exist; pass through to lending rates is immediate but incomplete; exchange rate pass through is significant under the peg | NRB WP 17; Byanjankar 2025; ScienceDirect 2025 papers |
| Peg and autonomy | Contested: Budha 2015 reports long run price convergence with substantial policy independence; a 2025 paper reports inflation anchored to India and lost autonomy | Budha 2015; ScienceDirect 2025 |
| Real time data | Rule estimates on revised data can mislead | Orphanides 1998 |
| Political uncertainty | Priced in financial markets; associated with lower investment in Nepal; for RBI decisions around elections only press commentary was found, no peer reviewed study | Kelly, Pastor and Veronesi 2016; World Bank Nepal ICA |

## Candidate contributions (to be confirmed or dropped)

1. **India to Nepal policy spillover at the decision level.** No study found so far measures whether RBI decisions predict NRB decisions or the Nepali interbank rate, beyond Nepali domestic conditions. This is the strongest candidate. Before claiming it, strands D and E must be searched in Google Scholar, NepJOL and IMF Article IV Selected Issues for Nepal.
2. **A sealed five year blind test of RBI decision prediction that compares rule based, ordered choice and machine learning models on the same window.** Ordered probit studies exist for other central banks, and the Turkish ML study exists, but no RBI study of this design was found. The design also tests whether the Garga and Sengupta hybrid rule holds out of sample across the 2022 tightening and the later easing.
3. **Political and policy events as predictors for RBI and NRB decisions, tested against a strict out of sample bar.** Only commentary was found for India. A negative result here would still be informative.
4. **A peg aware suggestion engine for Nepal** that represents the peg as a constraint and reports when NRB deviates from what Indian conditions would imply.

## What would remove a candidate

* Contribution 1 is removed if a prior study is found that estimates RBI to NRB or Indian to Nepali short rate spillovers at monthly or decision frequency.
* Contribution 2 is weakened if an RBI decision classification study with a genuine out of sample test is found; it would then become a replication and extension.
* Contribution 3 is removed if a peer reviewed study of electoral or political effects on RBI or NRB decisions is found; the project would then replicate it.
